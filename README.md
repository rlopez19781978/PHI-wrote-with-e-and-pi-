## Abstract

This paper presents a novel analytical expression for the golden ratio ($\phi$), designated as the **López Rodríguez Identity**. By combining foundational mathematical constants—namely Euler's number ($e$), the circle constant ($\pi$), and standard integers—the structure achieves an extraordinary absolute error of approximately $3,71 \times 10^{-30}$. This work details the formulation, structural significance, and error analysis of the proposed identity.

## 1. Introduction

The golden ratio, defined algebraically as:

$$\phi = \frac{1 + \sqrt{5}}{2} \approx 1.618033988749895...$$

has fascinated mathematicians for centuries due to its ubiquitous presence in geometry, nature, and number theory. While algebraic representations of $\phi$ are well-established, discovering rapidly converging transcendental expressions or mixed-constant identities that approximate $\phi$ with extreme precision remains an engaging pursuit in experimental mathematics.

The López Rodríguez Identity successfully integrates exponential towers, scaling factors, and transcendental bases into a single compact formulation.

## 2. The López Rodríguez Identity

The core proposition of the identity is expressed as follows:

$$\phi = \frac{e^{e^2}}{1000} + \frac{e^{e^e}} + \frac{1000}{\pi^{\sqrt{\pi}}}$$

### Component Breakdown

1. **The Primary Scaling Term:** $\frac{e^{e^2}}{10^3}$
Utilizes a double exponential tower scaled by a power of ten to establish the primary magnitude near the target value.
2. **The Exponential Correction Term:** $e^{-e^e}$
A rapidly decaying transcendental term that acts as a micro-adjustment.
3. **The Transcendental Balance Term:** $\frac{10^3}{\pi^{\sqrt{\pi}}}$
Combines the scaling factor with powers of $\pi$ and its square root to stabilize the lower-order discrepancies.

## 3. Error Analysis and Precision

Rigorous numerical evaluation of the identity yields a remarkable level of accuracy. When compared against the true value of the golden ratio, the absolute error is bounded by:

$$\text{Error} \approx 3,71 \times 10^{-30}$$

This level of precision—accurate to nearly 30 decimal places—confirms that the structural interplay between $e$, $\pi$, and the integer scaling factors is not random, but reflects a deep mathematical harmony within the selected operational framework.
